## The finding

The express arm measures five requirements and then hands over to the
ordinary ritual, which derives the tier for itself. The two derivations
are close but not the same: the eligibility asks whether a spec OWNS
each fenced path, and `classifyTier` asks whether a keeper PINS it —
which the ritual answers by grading the scoped suite. Where that suite
is red at the base, `classifyTier` returns standard, and the lane is cut
with a bench and a phase-one pass while the compact card carries the
line saying it was dispatched executor-only under the bounded contract.
The `verify.tier` switch reaches the same end by a shorter route: at
`guarded-for-every-card` every express card classifies guarded and the
classifier is never consulted.

Nothing reads the express label outside the withdrawal. No step of the
dispatch and no keeper of the merge asks whether a labelled card
actually took the road its label names, so the label is a claim about
the flow that the flow does not check.

Criterion 4 still holds as written — the flow runs the executor only AT
the bounded tier, and two bodies pin that with a non-bounded control.
What is missing is the binding between the label and the tier.

## What would settle it

Have the express run read the tier the ritual classified and refuse, by
name, when it is not bounded — unwinding as it already does for a
preflight refusal — or, where the seat wants the lane anyway, require
the label to come off by the withdrawal that exists for exactly that
shape. A body drives an express plan whose fence forces a standard
classification and requires one of the two.
