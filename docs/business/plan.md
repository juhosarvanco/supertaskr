# Supertaskr — business plan (draft v0, opened 2026-08-30)

Drafted by the marketing session under the 2026-08-30 rulings.
Everything here frames decisions for @human; nothing here is a
decision except where a dated ruling is cited. Feature statements
follow the version-planning room's standing rule — the v1 partition
is itself still a draft awaiting @human's markup, and this plan
inherits that draft status wherever it leans on the cut line.

## What the business is

An open-source method and a local-first app that run the entire
AI-native SDLC: a structured interview turns an idea into a fenced,
criteria-bearing board; agents the user already pays for build it in
parallel lanes; a different model returns binding verdicts; the
whole history is files in the user's repo. Identity constraints from
NORTH_STAR (non-negotiable, and each one is also a market position):
local-first, no accounts, no hosted backend, no telemetry; never in
the inference billing path; model-agnostic; terminal-forever;
solo-founder maintainable.

## Positioning (M3 ruling)

Lead with the full Supertaskr SDLC approach — the whole loop as the
story, the binding verdict as the sharpest proof inside it, the
self-hosted proof (Supertaskr built Supertaskr, records readable) as the
credibility floor. The competitive ground is the landed five-ring
map (docs/research/competitors.md, 2026-08-30): nobody else spans
idea → interview → exact tasks → governed multi-model execution →
provable record WITH a binding adversarial verdict; the trackers
that grew agents (Ring 1.5) have distribution but are all advisory
and all meter AI.

## Segments, in order

1. **The technical builder** (NORTH_STAR first user): solo founder
   with abandoned vibe-coded repos + developer with a months-old
   sediment session. Front door: terminal + repo + open-source
   method. This segment is the access-pass program's population.
2. **The non-coder client** (later, ADR-006): the guided, paid
   spec-studio layer inheriting an interview sharpened on segment 1.
3. **Teams, then enterprises** (v2+ horizon per the draft
   partition): fleet fences / team enablement; the enterprise raw
   material already exists in the rooms (compliance-for-free — the
   record chain rendering into SOC 2/ISO change-control evidence;
   org skill packs; the git-host access model). Air-gapped/regulated
   deployment is a PAID SEGMENT, not a hobbyist preference (Plane's
   SKU proves it — landed map, steal list).

## Go-to-market phases

- **Phase 0 — now:** build in public; open the access-pass WAITLIST
  early (P0/P1 rulings — collect interest before v1, promise
  positions not dates); occasional build-in-public notes keep the
  list warm and feed comparison-page SEO later.
- **Phase 1 — at v1 (T3 trigger):** the access-pass program runs
  (rolling, intake-throttled, 30-day windows); its metrics ARE the
  north-star metrics, so the program doubles as the
  riskiest-assumption experiment on outside users.
- **Phase 2 — launch:** the launch post under the M3 lead, armed
  with program numbers (median interview→board minutes, %
  dispatching within a week) and the map's verified competitor
  claims (re-verify all vendor claims before publishing — they
  churn monthly). Honest "Supertaskr vs X" comparison pages — the
  one-person distribution engine (steal list).
- **Phase 3 — the channel move** (landed map, conclusion 5): don't
  be a fourth tracker; BE THE AGENT the trackers delegate to.
  Linear's AgentSession, Agents in Jira, and Plane's @mentionable
  agents are one integration surface with three distributions, and
  Supertaskr returns the artifact none of them can produce: a
  reproducible REJECTED. (Strategy, not promise — any integration
  is a card that needs a version ruling; the M4 strategy room owns
  the question of how central this becomes.)

## Revenue frames (for @human — nothing decided)

Norms from the landed map: free single-user core; charge for
collaboration; ~$15–20/mo individual ceiling under visible pressure
from Plane at $6/seat; never meter AI (all three Ring-1.5 vendors
do — our non-goal is a price comparison now). Candidate paid
surfaces, each honestly annotated:

- **The interview / spec-studio layer** ("open-source the pipeline;
  sell the interview") — strongest fit with the ChatPRD existence
  proof ($15/mo, >100k users, solo founder); serves segment 2
  without taxing segment 1.
- **Team features** (fleet fences, team enablement) — v2 territory
  by the draft partition; the classic charge-for-collaboration
  line.
- **Enterprise evidence** (compliance-for-free rendered as paid
  exports/support) — furthest out, largest contracts, raw material
  already in the rooms.
- **Method packs marketplace** (charter entry 19) — horizon-tier;
  listed for completeness, not for planning.

The pricing DECISION waits for the v1 version sitting (what ships
free defines what can be paid). This file's next revision owes
@human 2–3 concrete pricing options once that sitting rules.

## Costs & operations

Near-zero infrastructure by design (no cloud, no hosted backend, no
inference costs — the user's own agent subscriptions do the
building). The real costs are @human's time (triage capacity is the
program's admitted bottleneck — the intake throttle prices it
honestly) and model spend on Supertaskr's own development (stamped per
card in the records; the seat-economics series makes even our costs
quotable marketing material).

## Metrics (the applause-metrics exclusion applies)

North-star criteria only: outside users dispatching real work
within a week; interview→dispatchable-board minutes; cold-start
pass rate; drift incidents falling. Deliberately absent: stars,
signups, traffic. The waitlist's length is an operations number
(throttle input), never a success claim.

## Risks

- **Platform absorption (Ring 0):** the official platforms grow the
  middle of our workflow. Mitigation is structural: file-based,
  model-agnostic — their improvements lower our dispatch cost; the
  interview/beginning is the defensible ground.
- **Category mortality (Ring 1 graveyard):** mitigated by identity
  — local-first files mean Supertaskr can vanish and users lose
  nothing; now a supporting clause, still true.
- **The riskiest assumption** (planning theater): if a thorough
  plan doesn't keep agents coherent over months, the product is a
  beautiful board over nothing. The access-pass program is the
  outside-user experiment; drift metrics per milestone are the
  leading indicators.
- **Atlassian adds a blocking mode:** the single likeliest external
  event to blunt the narrowed claim's "binding" clause (their
  one-vendor structure makes adversarial hard, but binding is one
  toggle). Watch item in the landed map; re-verify before every
  launch material.
- **Solo bus factor:** mitigated by the succession guarantee — the
  method's own cold-start discipline is also the business's
  continuity plan.
