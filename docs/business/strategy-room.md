## The question (from the 2026-08-25 addendum, now the landed map's
first open item)

*If the rings are being spanned by incumbents, is the integration
still the product — or is adversarial verification the product, with
the board as its delivery vehicle?*

The old map's conclusion 1 said the integration is the product. That
rested on nobody spanning the rings — and Linear (Intake → Plan →
Agents → Coding Sessions → Diffs) and Jira/Rovo Dev (work item →
plan → generation → review) now both do, verified 2026-08-30. The
answer changes what milestone 4 and the F-04 slice are FOR: either
the board is the destination, or the board is what makes the verdict
legible while the verdict is what people come for.

## The options

- **A — integration-is-the-product.** The full loop is the offer;
  the verdict is its sharpest feature. Milestone 4's board surfaces
  are the destination; the channel move (registering as an agent
  against Linear/Jira/Plane protocols) is a distribution tactic.
- **B — verification-is-the-product.** The reproducible REJECTED is
  the offer; the board is packaging. Milestone 4 optimizes for the
  verdict's legibility; the channel move is the strategy, and the
  standalone app is one delivery vehicle among several.
- **C — sequenced:** A for v1 (the loop must exist whole to prove
  anything), B as the expansion thesis (the verdict is what travels
  into other people's trackers).

## This session's read (drafting position, not a vote that counts)

C. The M3 ruling already tells the v1 story as the full loop, and
the loop must run whole before any of its organs can be sold
separately. But the expansion bet — what makes the trackers'
distribution a channel instead of a threat — is the verdict
traveling alone. Holding both, in that order, keeps milestone 4 as
scoped today while committing the strategy room to revisit when the
channel move becomes buildable.

## Resolution

**RULED 2026-08-30, @human, in session: A — INTEGRATION IS THE PRODUCT.**

The full loop is the offer; the verdict is its sharpest feature.
Milestone 4's board surfaces are the destination.

**@human's own reasoning, which narrows the ruling further than the
option text does:** *"I'm not sure why i would want to build a Jira or
Linear integration, as our product is so similar."* So the channel move
— registering as an agent against Linear/Jira/Plane protocols — is NOT
adopted even as the distribution tactic option A allows. Option A's text
kept it as a tactic; this ruling declines it on the ground that a
product this similar to the incumbents does not gain by entering their
surface as a guest.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT DECIDE, said plainly so nobody reads more into it
than was ruled:** the verdict's value as the differentiator is untouched
— it is what makes the loop worth buying, and the 27% first-pass
rejection evidence stands. What was rejected is the DELIVERY VEHICLE
question (should the verdict travel alone into other people's trackers),
not the verdict's importance. If the incumbents' similarity later
becomes a distribution problem rather than a positioning one, that is
the event that reopens this room.
